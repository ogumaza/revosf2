A feature release: revosf2 converts the music and sound effects of Wii games that play their sound through JAudio2, the sound library in Nintendo EAD's JSystem. Twilight Princess and Link's Crossbow Training are two of them. Sound archives convert as in 1.1.0.

Requires 64-bit Windows 10 or later, with no additional runtime DLLs. Drop one or more `.brsar` or `.baa` files, game folders or disc images onto `revosf2.exe`, or use a terminal. `revosf2 --help` lists the options. Output goes in a folder beside each input.

For macOS on Apple Silicon, follow the build instructions in the README. macOS builds include `Revosf2.app`. Drop archives onto the app in Finder to convert them. The app takes only `.brsar` and `.baa` files. Convert games from a terminal.

## New

- **JAudio2 games.** Some Wii games have no sound archive. They play their sound through JAudio2 instead. Give revosf2 the game, or the game's start-up archive (`.baa`) with the `Seqs` and `Waves` folders beside it. Given the game, revosf2 puts the files in a folder named after the start-up archive, inside `<game name>_revosf2`. The sequences come from the game's sound table. The files take the names that the table gives the sounds. Sound effects are sequences too.
- **Options for JAudio2 games.** `--loops`, `--seed` and `--only` work as for a sound archive. The game's random state starts at 0 unless `--seed` gives another. Ports start at 0, as in the game. `--var` and `--dol` change nothing in a JAudio2 game.

## Known limitations

As in 1.1.0, apart from the changes above. JAudio2 times its music for the GameCube's rate of about 32,028 Hz. The Wii plays it at 32,000 Hz. There the music plays 0.089% slower and 1.5 cents lower than in the files. The fitted envelopes can't follow an oscillator's table that rises again or loops. Random instrument effects play at their base values. Tremolos, track filters, pitch envelopes and notes on the DSP's built-in oscillators are left out. A vibrato deeper than a semitone is kept to a semitone. revosf2 reports each of these for each sequence. It keeps every note. See "JAudio games" and "Differences from the game" in the README for the full list.
