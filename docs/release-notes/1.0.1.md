A bug-fix release: loud tracks, changing vibratos, tempos in loops and the low-pass of later games now follow the game more closely. Convert an archive again to get the corrected files.

| File | Contents |

| `revosf2-1.0.1-win-x64.zip` | Windows x64 executable, `README.md`, `LICENSE` and `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` |

Requires 64-bit Windows 10 or later, with no additional runtime DLLs. The executable is now built with clang-cl instead of MSVC. Drop one or more `.brsar` files onto `revosf2.exe`, or use a terminal. `revosf2 --help` lists the options. Output goes in a folder beside each archive.

For macOS on Apple Silicon, follow the build instructions in the README. Linux and Intel Macs are no longer supported. macOS builds include `Revosf2.app`. Drop archives onto the app in Finder to convert them.

## Fixes

- **Loud tracks.** A track's volumes and velocities can go above the range of MIDI's velocity and volume controllers. The game still plays the track's notes up to full level. Each note's preset now raises its zones by the part beyond that range, up to full level. Such notes used to play too quietly. A note whose level still differs from the game's by more than 0.1 dB is reported.
- **Vibratos that change during a note.** A note whose pitch LFO changes while it sounds gets a MIDI channel of its own. The channel's pitch bends follow the game's LFO in every sound frame. Such a note used to keep the LFO settings of its start. It still does when no channel is free. revosf2 reports such notes.
- **Tempo in loops.** A tempo change on a loop's first tick now comes after the loop marker. A player that loops then plays every pass at that tempo. Playback without looping is the same as before.
- **Gliding release tails.** A release tail stops bending its MIDI channel once it has faded 40 dB below full level. The game glides each note separately. Notes that share the channel no longer bend with a tail that can't be heard.
- **Main loops.** A loop that a track runs inside a pass of another loop, such as a countdown of one-tick waits between notes, no longer becomes the main loop. A player looping on the markers used to play a silent tick there. The game lets a track that waits a negative number of ticks wait forever. Such a track no longer blocks the choice of the main loop.
- **Notes in a crowded tick.** revosf2 keeps every note that a track starts in one tick, up to 1,024. It used to keep only as many as AX has voices: 96, or 64 in the builds of 2006.
- **The low-pass of later games.** The sound library's builds from November 2008 on take the track low-pass's cutoff from a table. The table's steps are about a third of an octave apart, from 80 Hz to 12.8 kHz. revosf2 now does the same when the game's `main.dol` shows such a build. A setting between two steps takes the lower step's cutoff. The lowest settings give 80 Hz. The older builds' formula goes down to 31 Hz. Without `main.dol`, an archive still plays by its version.
- **Tremolos above full level.** While a note's envelope is at full level, the upper half of its tremolo changes nothing in the game. The SoundFont and SFZ LFOs raise the level there. revosf2 now reports such notes.
- **Silent parts.** MIDI files leave out the events of a part that plays no note.
- **Damaged sequences.** A variable-length argument that runs past the end of the sequence data now ends there, rather than reading on.

## Known limitations

As in 1.0.0, apart from the fixes above. See "Differences from the game" in the README for the full list.
